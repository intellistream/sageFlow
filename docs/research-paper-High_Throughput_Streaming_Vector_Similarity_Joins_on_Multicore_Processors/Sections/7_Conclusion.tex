\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied VSJoin as a multicore streaming vector-similarity join path and evaluated it under sliding-window semantics. In our implementation and evaluated workloads, the results support three bounded conclusions: (i) bounded-staleness read/write decoupling (P1) improves the quality--efficiency operating region under update churn, (ii) budgeted boundary coverage routing (P2) is more reliable than strict unicast and less wasteful than broad broadcast-like fanout, and (iii) predictable logical remapping with bounded control actions (P3) improves skew robustness while keeping control overhead manageable.

The experiments also identify explicit degradation boundaries rather than universal wins: overly aggressive control settings can increase churn, extreme fanout budgets can either under-cover boundaries or inflate duplicates, and rebuild intervals at either extreme can hurt freshness--overhead balance.

Our future work directly follows these observed limitations: (1) improve adaptive controllers for rebalance and rebuild to reduce control-churn sensitivity, (2) derive tighter quality/cost bounds for budgeted routing and bounded staleness, and (3) extend evaluation to broader real-world drift patterns and hardware settings. We do not claim global optimality beyond tested configurations.
